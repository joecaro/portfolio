## The integration

A Fortune 100 bank uses our voice agents to call customers about overdue payments. The agent verifies who answered, explains the balance, records a payment or promise to pay, and can hand the call to a person.

The integration had to support the bank's contact-center lifecycle as well as its network and security controls: sign in, become ready, handle a call, and complete wrap-up before becoming available again. Transfer and disconnect events must stay associated with the right session. These are the workflow requirements; each implementation needs separate validation.

That lifecycle crosses software we own and a contact-center interface we integrate with. Staging verified the CTI client's login and readiness transitions. We also built a contact-center simulator around the event contract so the agent can be exercised before changes enter the bank's release process.

## A state transition that mattered

Call arrival, disconnect and readiness are related events, but they are not interchangeable. A simulator fix addressed a cleanup edge case: a call remains in progress until the matching ready event arrives. The simulator keeps that agent blocked from new calls until its readiness event is delivered, and preserves agent-specific events during deduplication. A synthetic flow checked delayed readiness, call disposal, in-progress blocking and duplicate-ready handling.

The staging CTI integration separately verified ten agents progressing through initialization, login, not-ready and ready. Those checks establish startup and readiness behavior; they do not establish a production transfer outcome. Transfers remain part of the integration boundary and must be validated against the contact-center behavior they invoke.

## What the environment required

The product was designed for our cloud, while this deployment runs inside the bank's environment. The bank controls access to production, networking, deployment approvals and model access. That meant we built and debugged against staging, feedback from the bank's engineers, and artifacts returned through its release process. Models were accessed through the bank's approved route, and speech synthesis ran offline.

Technical alignment began in May 2025, with production cutover at the end of August. The card line went live in September 2025; auto loans were added in July 2026.

## Making a timeout reproducible

In September 2026, the Live Agents screen began timing out. The no-event polling response still included growing event history for each agent. I tested whether that oversized response could add pressure to the reader and API by reproducing the large rows in staging, then comparing the previous and optimized projections with the same data and load.

The response fell from 2,514,826 bytes to 38,100 bytes. With 50 concurrent viewers making a burst of requests, p95 latency fell from 4.402 seconds to 0.291 seconds. With 50 viewers polling eight times each, p95 fell from 3.142 seconds to 0.502 seconds; successful responses changed from 399 of 400 to 400 of 400.

This controlled staging comparison showed that removing unused history from the polling response removed payload-driven reader/API backpressure for that fixture and workload. Staging did not reproduce the full production fleet, polling cadence or incident conditions, so this result does not establish that oversized rows were the only cause of the production outage.

## The cost of the first rollout

The initial delivery used a bank-specific version of the platform. That let us adapt quickly, but duplicated modules and environment-specific conditionals made fixes harder to carry between the bank and our shared product. Consolidation is still in progress: the goal is one configurable codebase rather than maintaining the same behavior in two places.

## Model review and releases

The bank's reviewers needed a way to inspect calls and assess model changes. The system produces call-level exports and automated evaluation results; sampled calls can also be reviewed by people, with human judgments compared against the automated evaluator. We ran fixed evaluation campaigns so a targeted change could be checked against a stable baseline, and compared models on the same frozen evaluations.

I worked directly with the bank's reviewers, demonstrated the review tooling and converted their feedback into engineering work. I also helped refine the model-risk inventory so after-call evaluation was described as evidence for review, not as a control that gates live output.

Releases pass through the bank's security scan and approval process. I used exact image versions and digests to verify that the artifact approved for deployment matched the artifact we built, and kept a separate release record so we could establish what was intended to run.

Business-performance results are measured by the bank and remain private.
